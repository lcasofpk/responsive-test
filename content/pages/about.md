<h2>Background</h2>
<p style="max-width:850px;margin:0 auto;">The sustainability of local governments has long been a challenge in Pakistan. Although Article 140-A of the Constitution gives provincial governments a full mandate to ensure continuity of local government, this has repeatedly failed in practice — a failure for which national and provincial governments, together with the Election Commission of Pakistan, share responsibility.</p>
<p style="max-width:850px;margin:16px auto 0;">In 2015, the Association for Development of Local Governance (ADLG) was formed to take up this challenge on a permanent, national footing. ADLG is an alliance of the four Provincial Local Councils Associations of Pakistan — Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan — and has since grown into the national voice of local governments, with Azad Jammu &amp; Kashmir, Gilgit-Baltistan, and the Metropolitan Corporation Islamabad joining the alliance. In effect, every local government in Pakistan — from Village Panchayats and Neighbourhood and Union Councils to Tehsil and District Councils, Town, Municipal, and Metropolitan Corporations — is a member of ADLG through its provincial and regional associations.</p>
</section>
<section class="section section-alt">
<h2>Mission &amp; Objectives</h2>
<div class="pillars">
<div class="pillar-card"><h3>Mission</h3><p>ADLG wants to enhance the quality of life for everyone in Pakistan by rejuvenating democracy and giving people real reasons to participate in civic life and their communities — making participation in local elections a must-do, with a meaningful citizen vote on spending decisions. This means significant devolution of power from provinces to the local level.</p></div>
<div class="pillar-card"><h3>Objectives</h3><p>Effective public participation in local governance and decision-making; capacity building of local governments and inter-tier cooperation; solving common community development issues; liaison with stakeholders; promoting local government interests in policy and legislation; knowledge-sharing locally and internationally; and building linkages with regional and international local government bodies.</p></div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="section">
<h2>Area of Work</h2>
<div class="pillars">
<div class="pillar-card"><h3>Access to Basic Services</h3><p>Local governments worldwide are judged on their ability to deliver quality public services — water, sanitation, health, basic education, waste management, transport, and energy. ADLG focuses on the governance of these services, identifying local authorities' specific needs and proposing recommendations that align with the UN Sustainable Development Goals.</p></div>
<div class="pillar-card"><h3>Decentralization &amp; Local Democracy</h3><p>ADLG works to strengthen decentralization and local self-government nationwide, promoting effective devolution, lobbying for consistency in local government elections, and advocating the adoption of international principles on decentralization.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="pillars" style="margin-top:24px;">
<div class="pillar-card"><h3>Communication, Education &amp; Public Awareness</h3><p>ADLG promotes efficient communication between local governments, upper tiers of government, and citizens. Believing that elected representatives can only work effectively when well informed about their roles, ADLG organizes training programmes for both elected representatives and officials, and works with communities — particularly in under-served rural and urban areas — to create demand for improved municipal services.</p></div>
</div>
</section>

<p style="max-width:850px;margin:24px auto 0;text-align:center;color:#555;">ADLG's Executive Committee and 32-member Board of Governors are elected by the member Local Councils Associations at the Association's Biannual General Meeting. Through its member LCAs, ADLG has outreach in every district of Pakistan, where each District Executive Committee comprises 30 councillors, with 33% women's representation guaranteed at every level.</p>
